# Website Builder: observed client test

Status: prepared; no client sessions have been conducted. Automated browser checks are engineering evidence, not customer research.

Recruit three clients who normally update their website: one occasional editor, one frequent content editor, and one person who approves or publishes changes. Each session takes about 20 minutes. Record only with the participant's agreement.

## Preparation

Use a staging copy of the client's website, with their usual permissions and at least two pages sharing a footer. Include a sample image and one approved brand style. Confirm that the staging publishing destination works before the session. Keep the production site out of this exercise.

Give the participant the URL and their account. Say: "We are testing the builder, not you. Please say what you expect to happen and what you are looking for. Work as you normally would."

Do not demonstrate the editor first. Let the built-in first-edit guide appear. Observe how the participant uses or dismisses it. If they ask for help, ask what they expected to find; provide help only after recording the difficulty.

## Tasks

1. **Change a headline.** Ask the participant to replace the homepage headline with supplied text. Observe selection, editing and whether they recognise that saving a draft does not publish it. Target: two minutes without help.
2. **Replace an image.** Ask them to choose the supplied image, add a description and keep the subject visible in a square crop. Have them check the phone preview. Target: three minutes without help.
3. **Apply an approved brand style.** Ask them to apply the provided button style to a selected button. For a manager, also review the affected pages in Settings and save the reviewed changes as drafts. Observe whether scope and pending publication are understood.
4. **Change a shared footer.** Ask them to change the footer text on both pages. Before they apply the edit, ask which pages they expect to change. Observe whether they find the shared review and distinguish a shared change from a page-only edit.
5. **Review and publish to staging.** Ask them to check their changes and publish. Observe whether they wait for live verification, use the live-site link and inspect the result. Target: two minutes of interaction, excluding deployment time.
6. **Recover a mistake.** Ask them to undo an edit and locate the previous published version. Do not require an unplanned rollback.

After each task ask: "How easy or difficult was that, from 1 (very difficult) to 7 (very easy)?" At the end ask: "What would stop you using this without help?"

## Decisions after all three sessions

- Fix any instance where a participant publishes unintended content, misunderstands shared scope, or cannot recover an edit before wider rollout.
- Prioritise the same obstacle appearing in two or more sessions.
- Aim for all participants to complete the headline, image and publish tasks without coaching. Report actual counts alongside timings; three people do not establish a population-wide success rate.
- Record each finding as: observed behaviour, consequence, proposed change, and a repeat task to check the change.
- Repeat only the affected tasks after fixes. Keep engineering checks and participant results separate.
